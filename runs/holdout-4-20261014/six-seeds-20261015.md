# Run `holdout-4-20261014` addendum 3 — six CHTD seeds: the data-efficiency claim, halved

Written 2025-10-15 01:30 after training three new student-state seeds (3/4/5) and evaluating all
six on the h1 holdout. The previous record claimed, from one evaluation, that CHTD "reaches the
33-row teacher-demo point's failure count on 9 student-state rows". Six seeds turn that point into
a distribution — and the claim survives on one endpoint and fails on the other.

**The findings, stated first.**

1. **All six seeds beat the base on failures.** Failure counts 1–3 of 15 against the base's 5
   (six judge readings of one deterministic answer set: 4–5). Pooled over 90 pair-cells the
   ordinal delta against the base is **+0.200, t = +2.23** — stronger than the single-seed
   estimate it replaces (+0.170, t = 1.29).
2. **The seed spread is small — smaller than the measurement noise around one seed.** Ordinal
   scores across six seeds: 1.400–1.600, **sd 0.084**. The same adapter evaluated twice (v2 vs
   v2b) moves by **0.333**, four times as much. The recipe is stable across seeds; a single
   evaluation of any one seed is not.
3. **The data-efficiency claim holds on the failure endpoint and fails on the ordinal one.**
   Against v1 (33 teacher-demo rows, 2/15 failures, ordinal 1.667):
   - **failures: 5 of 6 seeds are at or below v1's count** (1, 1, 2, 2, 2 vs 2; one seed at 3);
   - **ordinal mean: 0 of 6 seeds reach v1's 1.667** (best 1.600).
   So on the endpoint the design targets (errors avoided), nine student-state rows match thirty-three
   teacher-demonstration rows; on graded answer quality overall they do not.

## The measurements

Training: `distill/finetune.py` on the same 9-row dataset (`97a88c73b175…`), same recipe as seeds
0–2 (rank 32, alpha 64, lr 2e-4, 30 epochs, 60 steps, 8192 tokens, `supervise='last'`), only the
seed differing. Wall clock ~55 minutes for three, run in parallel on one RTX 4090. Losses
1.0295 / 1.0212 / 0.9995 (seeds 0–2) against 1.0–1.1 for the new three — same regime.

Evaluation: `eval/holdout_sweep.py` (the shared-home flow), all six seeds on the **same 15-task
holdout**, each seed served from its own port; 90 answers, all graded blind by the validated

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Establishes.**

- **The student-state recipe is seed-stable on this task set** (sd 0.084 across six seeds) and
  every seed improves on the base — 6/6 on the failure endpoint, pooled t = +2.23.
- **Data efficiency on the failure endpoint**: 9 rows ≈ 33 rows in errors avoided (5 of 6 seeds at
  or below the 33-row count).
- **The measurement floor, again**: comparing two trained arms on one 15-task evaluation each is
  uninformative — the re-evaluation noise (0.333) is four times the seed-to-seed spread (0.084).
  Every single-evaluation arm-to-arm comparison in the earlier ladder records carries that caveat
  and this record sharpens it.

**Does not establish.**

- **Data efficiency on overall answer quality.** On the ordinal mean no CHTD seed reaches the
  33-row point; the gap (1.533 vs 1.667) is 0.133, inside the re-evaluation noise but consistent
  in direction across all six seeds, so it should be read as a real (small) deficit, not noise.
- **That the failure endpoint is what the grader weights.** It is the endpoint the design's named
  capability (error recovery) predicts, and it was strengthened by pre-registering it — but in this
  record it was chosen after seeing the pooled h1/h2/h3 data.
- **Anything about the dose ladder.** Three dose points remain unresolvable at 15 tasks; this run
  changed the seed count, not the task count.

## What the six seeds change for the next run

1. **Report seed spread, not single evaluations.** With the spread measured (0.084) and the
   evaluation noise measured (0.333), the affordable design is clear: more tasks matter more than
   more seeds for arm-to-arm comparisons, and any arm compared on 15 tasks needs at least a
   re-evaluation.
2. **The confirmatory question stands as computed** (`eval/confirmatory_power.py`): ~110 tasks/arm
   for the failure-rate endpoint at the observed effect.
3. **The honest headline from the whole programme, with six seeds behind it**: fine-tuning on
   nine student-state rows cuts the base's flat wrong-answer rate by roughly two-thirds
   (5/15 → 1.8/15 mean), stably across seeds, with the same rows reaching the error-avoidance level
   of a 33-row teacher-demonstration arm — while trailing it slightly on overall answer quality.
